The choice of a plasmonic metasurface of disordered spherical AuNPs for biosensing was motivated by its cost-effectiveness and rapid fabrication process compared to ordered nanoparticle arrays or AuNPs with more complex geometries. To design the biosensing-aimed metasurface with the desired morphological characteristics, the mean radii $a$ of the AuNPs and the surface coverage $\Theta$ of the metasurface have to be determined. These parameters  must ensure that the optical response of the metasurface lies within the visible spectrum, as biosensing probes can be damaged by ultraviolet light and are typically immersed in aqueous media, which exhibit significant absorption in the infrared region.

Two different designs of plasmonic metasurfaces are proposed based on the previous considerations, and on the available fabrication and measurement capabilities. The values of $a$ and $\Theta$ were  determined through a parametric variation procedure using reflectivity spectra calculations performed with the CSM and the TFT formalisms, each exploiting their modeling strengths. For the CSM-based design, the values $a = 30$~nm and $\Theta = 0.12$ were chosen, corresponding to AuNPs beyond the dipolar approximation in relatively sparse  ensembles. In contast, TFT-based  design resulted in $a=17.5$~nm and $\Theta=0.275$, suggesting smaller AuNPs forming denser ensembles.

In Fig. \sfref{fig:TD-R}{a)} the reflectivity  at an angle of incidence $\theta_i = 72^\circ$ of the proposed metasurfaces is presented as function of the incident wavelength $\lambda$ within the visible spectra for $p$ (continuous lines)  and $s$ polarized (dashed lines) light. The upper and lower panels in Fig. \ref{fig:TD-R} correspond to calculations with the CSM and the TFT with their corresponding parameters for the proposed metasurfaces, respectively, employing a substrate with a refractive index $n_\text{s} = 1.5$. Each curve in Fig. \ref{fig:TD-R} corresponds to a different refractive index $n_\text{m}$ of the matrix, as specified by the color map, considering  water as the reference matrix ---with a refractive index of $n_\text{m} = 1.33$--- and changing up to $n_\text{m} = 1.36$. The shaded regions in Fig. \sfref{fig:TD-R}{a)} highlights the spectral window of $500$~nm $\leq \lambda \leq 590$~nm and their manifications are shown in Fig.  \sfref{fig:TD-R}{b)}, enabling a better visualization of spectral shifts induced by variations in the matrix refractive index.

From its theoretical design, the proposed metasurfaces fulfill the spectral behavior requirements for biosensing applications. On the one hand, the metasurface designed under the CSM conditions(upper panels) exhibits a resonances at wavelengths near $580$~nm for $p$ polarization and near $570$~nm for $s$ polarization or all considered values of $n_\text{m}$.  On the other, the metasurfaces proposed under the TFT features present the resonances at approximately  $520$~nm and $525$~nm for $p$ and $s$ polarization respectively. For both metasurfaces and polarizations, a track of the minima spectral shift can be done, enabling the implementation of an intensity-based sensing scheme. However, the the broad resonance  widths observed in Fig. \sfref{fig:TD-R}{b)} reduce the quality factor of the simulated sensing mechanism, potentially limiting the sensitivity of the measurements. As an alternative, a phase-based sensing scheme based on the Topological Darkness (TD) phenomenon and on ellipsometric techniques offers improved quality factors.

The TD phenomenon refers to the occurrence of zero-reflection at specific spectral points in metasurfaces, arising from destructive interference or mode coupling induced by their nanostructured elements. The term TD reflects  the robustness of the  light suppression condition against perturbations, due to topological constraints present in the reciprocal space of the metasurface's optical responsem thus  making metasurfaces exhibiting TD highly attractive for sensing applications. While the TD has been primarly studied in ordered systems identifying the zero-reflection, it has also been demonstrated in disordered metasurfaces considering a near zero-reflection condition instead. Moreover, the TD phenomenon is associated with abrupt phase changes in the reflected light spectra  at the zero-reflection wavelength, which shifts with changes of the refractive index of the probe matrix, as seen in Fig.  \sfref{fig:TD-R}{b)}. 

The phase change of the light reflected by a metasurface can be determine with the spectra of the  ellipsometric parameters $\psi$ and $\Delta\phi$ defined as
%
\begin{align}
	{r^{(p)}}/{r^{(s)}} = \tan\psi \exp(i \Delta\phi)
\label{eq:ellipso}
\end{align}
%
where $r^{(j)}$ are the reflection amplitude coefficient for the $j$ polarization state. The $\Delta\phi$ parameter corresponds to the phase difference between the reflected $p$ and $s$ polarized components of light. The experimental determination of $\Delta\phi$ can be performed with the setup  described in Section \ref{s:Setup} in Fig. \ref{fig:setup-atr} by placing a birefringent crystal between the first linear polarizer and the prism supporting the metasurface with the crystal's optical axis is oriented at $45^\circ$ with respect to polarizer's axis as described in Ref [].

Fig. \sfref{fig:TD}{a)} presents the phase difference $\Delta\phi$  as a function of  the incident wavelength and the angle of incidence $\theta_i$  for the proposed metasurfaces to illustrating the TD phenomenon. The left (right) panel displays the calculated reciprocal  space of the the CSM-based (TFT-based) design considering a water matrix ($n_\text{m} = 1.33$). Spectral points exhibiting TD are signalized by the white loops with defined orientation. The white continuous and dashed lines indicate angular cuts at $\theta_i = 72^\circ$  corresponding to the phase difference curves shown in Fig. \sfref{fig:TD}{b)} using the same line styles.

The TD phenomenon can be identified for each panel in Fig. \sfref{fig:TD}{a)} based on the following features. First, the near zero-reflectance condition is fulfilled at two spectral points (see white loops) for each  mestasurface design, as indicated by the reflectivity spectra at $\theta_i = 72^\circ$ in Fig. \ref{fig:TD-R} and in concistency with Eq. \eqref{eq:ellipso} when $r^{(p)}\to 0$. In addition, $\Delta\phi$ at these spectral points becomes not well-defined, exhibiting an abrupt phase change as observed in the angular cuts in  Fig. \sfref{fig:TD}{b)}. The third TD feature observed in the theoretical results is the zero net topological charge (TC) in the phase space of the metasurfaces, obtained by adding the $\pm 1$ TC associated with each loop in each panel from Fig. \sfref{fig:TD}{a)}. The vanishing net TC ensures that any angular cut crossing the bright region between the TD points in  Fig.  \sfref{fig:TD}{b)} will display an inflection point in the phase difference, as exemplified in Fig. \sfref{fig:TD}{b)}. This property enables a robust and stable phase-based sensing mechanism.

In Fig. \ref{fig:TD-Sens} it is presented the phase-based sensing scheme with the proposed metasurfaces for a varying refractive index $n_\text{m}$ of the matrix at two angles of incidence: $70^\circ$ (blue gradient) and $72^\circ$ (red gradient). The upper panels correspond to the CSM-based design and the lower ones to the TFT-based design. Fig. \sfref{fig:TD-Sens}{a)}  shows the phase difference $\Delta\phi$ as a function of the incident wavelength $\lambda$ where each curve correspond to different values of $n_\text{m}$. In Fig. \sfref{fig:TD-Sens}{b)} the spectral derivative of this ellipsometric parameter is calculated, that is, $\dv*{\Delta\phi}{\lambda}$, whose maxima indicate the occurrence of TD at a specific wavelength $\lambda^\text{TD}$. The spectral shift $\delta\lambda^\text{TD}$ in response to changes in $n_\text{m}$ is indicated by arrows. Lastly, in Fig. \sfref{fig:TD-Sens}{c)} the spectral  shift $\delta\lambda^\text{TD}$ of the metasurfaces (continuous lines) is shown as a function of $n_\text{m}$ for the two selected angles of incidence with the different color gradients. For comparison, the theoretical spectral shift of the Surface Plasmon Polariton (SPP) excited in a $50$~nm thick continuous gold film is shown in dashed lines\footnote{The inclusion of the SPP spectral shift in Fig. \sfref{fig:TD-Sens}{c)} serves only as a reference, since the resonance wavelength of the SPP does not originate from the TD phenomenon, thus the symbol $\delta\lambda^\text{TD}$ becomes an abuse of notation for the SPP.}.
