The Thin Film Theory (TFT) is a MST-based framework suited to describe the electromagnetic  properties, in the far-field regime, of thin stratified regions ~\cite{bedeaux_optical_2004}. The TFT returns an approximated solution to Maxwell's equations, similarly to Fresnel's equations, for two semi-infinite media enclosing a region of the space, hereby identified as a thin film, with a previously unspecified composition~\cite{bedeaux_optical_2004,cuanalo_phase_2024,svedendahl_fano_2012}. The optical properties of the thin film, under the excess field formalism, are determined by introducing a polarization and magnetization  fields on the film through  constitutive equations \cite{bedeaux_optical_2004}. For example, the thin film can model an  ensemble of nanoparticles  given their material and density within the array~\cite{haarmans_optical_1995,lazzari_granfilm_2002}. The excess field approach modifies Maxwell's equations at the interfaces of the system, thus determining explicitly the discontinuities of the electromagnetic field at the boundaries of the thin film.

To study the reflection and transmission of an electromagnetic plane wave illuminating an arbitrary thin film, of thickness $d$, centered at $z=0$ in between two media characterized by their bulk dielectric functions or refractive indices $\varepsilon^\gtrless = (n^\gtrless)^2$, it is proposed that the total electric field $\vb{E}(\vb{r})$ is decomposed as follows~\cite{bedeaux_optical_2004,lazzari_granfilm_2002}
%
\begin{align}
	\vb{E}(\vb{r}) &= \vb{E}^{>}(\vb{r}) \Theta(z) + \vb{E}^{<}(\vb{r}) \Theta(-z) + \vb{E}^\text{excess}(\vb{r}),
	\label{eq:excessDef}
\end{align}
%
where the symbols $>$ and $<$ denote the semiinfinite media above and below the thin film, respectively. In Eq. \eqref{eq:excessDef} the time harmonic dependency in $\omega$ is implicitly included and $\Theta(z)$ is the Heaviside step function; the electric fields $\vb{E}^\gtrless$ are considered to be radiation fields~\cite{bedeaux_optical_2004}. The term $\vb{E}^\text{excess}(\vb{r})$ is the excess electric field and its contribution is non-zero in the neighborhood of the thin film since $\vb{E}(\vb{r}) \to  \vb{E}^{\gtrless}(\vb{r})$ when $z \to \pm \infty$. Thus, the whole effect of the thin film in the electromagnetic properties of the system can be described by considering the total excess electric field in the film $\vb{E}^\text{film}(\vb{r}, z = 0)$ in the limit when $d \to 0$, thus, yielding the expression~\cite{bedeaux_optical_2004,lazzari_granfilm_2002}
%
\begin{align}
	\vb{E}(\vb{r}) &= \vb{E}^{>}(\vb{r}) \Theta(z) + \vb{E}^{<}(\vb{r}) \Theta(-z) + \vb{E}^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel) \delta(z),
		\label{eq:ETotFilm}
\end{align}
%
with $\delta(z)$ the Dirac delta function and
%
\begin{align}
	\vb{E}^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel)
			= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \vb{E}^\text{excess}(\vb{r}) \dd{z}
			= \qty(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \vb{E}^\text{excess}(\vb{r})\exp(i k_z z) \dd{z})\eval_{k_z = 0},
	\label{eq:EFilm}
\end{align}
%
where $\vb{r}_\parallel$ is a vector evaluated at the plane $z=0$. In Eq. \eqref{eq:EFilm} it is explicitly shown that $	\vb{E}^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel)$ equals the Fourier transform of the excess electric field in the $z$ variable evaluated at $k_z = 0$. The same decomposition employed in Eqs. \eqref{eq:excessDef}--\eqref{eq:EFilm} for $\vb{E}(\vb{r})$ applies to the magnetic field $\vb{B}(\vb{r})$, as well as any other scalar or vector field relevant to the system~\cite{lazzari_granfilm_2002}, for example, the electric displacement field $\vb{D}(\vb{r})$, the $\vb{H}(\vb{r})$ field, and the external charge and external current densities, $\rho_\text{ext}(\vb{r})$  and $\vb{J}_\text{ext}(\vb{r})$. By substituting such definitions of the fields into Maxwell's equations, the following expressions for the excess quantities are obtained \cite{bedeaux_optical_2004}
%
\begin{subequations}%
\begin{align}
	\nabla\cdot\vb{D}^\text{excess}(\vb{r}) + \qty[D_\perp^>(\vb{r}_\parallel)-D_\perp^<(\vb{r}_\parallel)]\delta(z)  & = \rho^\text{excess}_\text{ext}(\vb{r}),\\
%	
	\nabla\cdot\vb{B}^\text{excess}(\vb{r}) + \qty[B_\perp^>(\vb{r}_\parallel)-B_\perp^<(\vb{r}_\parallel)]\delta(z)  & = 0,\\
%	
	\nabla\times\vb{E}^\text{excess}(\vb{r}) + \vu{e}_\perp \times \qty[\vb{E}^>_\parallel(\vb{r}_\parallel)-\vb{E}^<_\parallel(\vb{r}_\parallel)]\delta(z) & = +i\omega\vb{B}^\text{excess}(\vb{r}),\\
%	
	\nabla\times\vb{H}^\text{excess}(\vb{r}) + \vu{e}_\perp \times \qty[\vb{H}^>_\parallel(\vb{r}_\parallel)-\vb{H}^<_\parallel(\vb{r}_\parallel)]\delta(z) & = \vb{J}^\text{excess}_\text{ext}(\vb{r}) -i\omega\vb{D}^\text{excess}(\vb{r}),
\end{align}\label{eq:MaxwellExcess}%
\end{subequations}
%
where it was considered that the fields in the semi-infinite media are solution to Maxwell's equations and that the step and delta functions are related as $\dv*{\Theta(\pm z)}{z} = \pm \delta(z)$. The subscript $\parallel$ corresponds to the parallel components of the fields relative to the thin film, $\perp$ to their normal components to the film; $\vu{e}_\perp$ is the unitary vector in this direction. The terms in between brackets in Eqs. \eqref{eq:MaxwellExcess} are the boundary conditions of the electromagnetic fields evaluated at the thin film and they are modified from the known case of a sharp interface ---which yield Fresnel's equations--- by the excess quantities~\cite{bedeaux_optical_2004}.

The boundary conditions of the electromagnetic fields in the TFT formalism can be determined by calculating the Fourier transform  of Eqs. \eqref{eq:MaxwellExcess}  in the $z$ variable and then considering the particular case of $k_z = 0$, so that all excess quantities are interchanged for their total contribution according to Eq. \eqref{eq:EFilm}. Additionally,  total excess polarization $\vb{P}^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel)$ and magnetization $\vb{M}^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel)$ are defined in terms of the total excess electromagnetic fields, thus the boundary conditions in the absence of external sources ---$\vb{J}_\text{ext}(\vb{r})=\vb{0}$ and $\rho_\text{ext}(\vb{r})=0$--- can be written as~\cite{bedeaux_optical_2004,lazzari_granfilm_2002}
%
\begin{subequations}%
\begin{align}
		\qty[D_\perp^>(\vb{r}_\parallel)-D_\perp^<(\vb{r}_\parallel)]  & = -\nabla_\parallel\cdot\vb{P}_\parallel^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel),\\
		%	
		\qty[B_\perp^>(\vb{r}_\parallel)-B_\perp^<(\vb{r}_\parallel)]  & = -\nabla_\parallel\cdot\vb{M}_\parallel^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel),\\
		%	
		\qty[\vb{E}^>_\parallel(\vb{r}_\parallel)-\vb{E}^<_\parallel(\vb{r}_\parallel)] & = -i{\omega}\vu{e}_\perp\times \vb{M}^\text{film}_\parallel(\vb{r}_\parallel)-\nabla_\parallel P_\perp^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel),\\
		%	
		\qty[\vb{H}^>_\parallel(\vb{r}_\parallel)-\vb{H}^<_\parallel(\vb{r}_\parallel)] & = +i{\omega}\vu{e}_\perp\times \vb{P}^\text{film}_\parallel(\vb{r}_\parallel)-\nabla_\parallel M_\perp^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel),
\end{align}\label{eq:generalBC}%
\end{subequations}
%
where $\nabla_\parallel$ and $\nabla_\parallel\cdot$ are the bidimensional gradient and divergence operators on the thin film, respectively, and~\cite{bedeaux_optical_2004}
%
\begin{align}
	\vb{P}^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel) &= \vb{D}_\parallel^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel) - \varepsilon_0 {E}_\perp^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel) \vu{e}_\perp,
	\label{eq:Pfilm}\\
%	
	\vb{M}^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel) &= \frac{1}{\mu_0}\vb{B}_\parallel^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel) -  {H}_\perp^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel) \vu{e}_\perp,
	\label{eq:Mfilm}
\end{align}
%
with $\varepsilon_0$ ($\mu_0$) the electric permittivity (magnetic permeability) of the vacuum. It is noteworthy that the definition of $\vb{P}^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel)$ in Eq. \eqref{eq:Pfilm} is not just a direct identification of the procedure from Eqs. \eqref{eq:MaxwellExcess} to Eqs. \eqref{eq:generalBC} but it is based on the non-continuity of $ \vb{D}_\parallel(\vb{r})$ and $E_\perp(\vb{r})$ across boundaries, which give rise to an excess polarization on the film unlike $D_\perp(\vb{r})$ and $\vb{E}_\parallel(\vb{r})$, whose continuity do not have such an effect on the system~\cite{bedeaux_optical_2004,lazzari_granfilm_2002}. An analogous argument is performed on the definition of the magnetization field in Eq. \eqref{eq:Mfilm}.

The boundary conditions for the electromagnetic fields, given in Eq. \eqref{eq:generalBC}, reduce to Fresnel’s equations when the film represents a planar sharp interface between two linear homogeneous media by setting $\vb{P}^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel) = \vb{M}^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel) = \vb{0}$~\cite{bedeaux_optical_2004}. To introduce a thin film with a different, but known, structure, the total excess polarization and magnetization [Eqs. \eqref{eq:Pfilm} and \eqref{eq:Mfilm}] can be described by constitutive equations as an expansion to different orders of the electromagnetic fields~\cite{bedeaux_optical_2004}. The particular case where the thin film corresponds to a collection of identical plasmonic scatterers, with a size where no magnetic response is expected, the general constitutive relations to Eqs.  \eqref{eq:Pfilm} and \eqref{eq:Mfilm}  can be expressed to a linear order of any component of total excess electric field or the total excess displacement field~\cite{lazzari_granfilm_2002,haarmans_optical_1995}. Since $\vb{E}^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel)$ and $\vb{D}^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel)$ are linearly dependent, the total excess polarization and magnetization can be expressed as~\cite{bedeaux_optical_2004,lazzari_granfilm_2002}
%
\begin{align}
	\vb{P}^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel) = 
			\gamma\,\big\langle\vb{E}^\text{film}_\parallel(\vb{r}_\parallel)\big\rangle +
			\beta \,\big\langle {D}^\text{film}_\perp(\vb{r}_\parallel)\big\rangle \vu{e}_\perp
	\qquad\text{and}\qquad
	\vb{M}^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel) = \vb{0}
\label{eq:P-TFT}
\end{align}
%
where $\gamma$ and $\beta$ are known as the film susceptibilities, which encodes the physical properties of the ensemble of scatterers, and where the operator $\langle\cdot\rangle$ corresponds to the average on the film given as $\big\langle a^\text{film} (\vb{r}_\parallel)\big\rangle~=~(1/2)[a^>(\vb{r}_\parallel) + a^<(\vb{r}_\parallel)]$, for either a scalar or vector quantity $a^\gtrless(\vb{r})$~\cite{lazzari_granfilm_2002,bedeaux_optical_2004}.

The TFT employs the excess field formalism to determine the optical response of a metasurface embedded in a matrix and supported on a substrate, as shown in Fig. \ref{fig:esquemaTFT}, both non-absorbing nor magnetic ($\mu^\gtrless=\mu_0$), by calculating amplitude reflection $r_\text{TFT}$ and transmission $t_\text{TFT}$ coefficients from Eqs. \eqref{eq:generalBC} and \eqref{eq:P-TFT}, considering that the thin film consists in a disordered ensemble of identical plasmonic spherical particles. For a configuration of internal illumination, the electric field below the thin film corresponds to the superposition of an incident and a reflected electromagnetic plane waves $\vb{E}^\text{inc}(\vb{r})$ and $\vb{E}_\text{r}(\vb{r})$ traveling in the $\vu{k}^\text{inc}$ and $\vu{k}^\text{r}$ directions, respectively, within the substrate characterized by the dielectric function $\varepsilon^< = \varepsilon_i = n_i^2$, while above the electric field is given by the plane wave $\vb{E}^\text{t}(\vb{r})$ traveling in  the $\vu{k}^\text{t}$ direction in the matrix with $\varepsilon^> = \varepsilon_t = n_t^2$. To determine the amplitude coefficients under the TFT the cases of an $s$ and a $p$ polarized incident electromagnetic wave are treated separately.

\begin{figure}[t!]\centering
	\includegraphics[scale = 1]{Fig-TFT.pdf}
	\caption{Diagram of the reflection of transmission of  \textbf{a)} an $s$ polarized and \textbf{b)} a $p$ polarized electromagnetic plane wave $\vb{E}^\text{inc}$ traveling in the $\vu{k}^\text{inc}$ direction at an angle of incidence $\theta_i$ that illuminates a collection of identical spherical particles from a medium characterized by the dielectric function $\varepsilon^< = \varepsilon_i$ (blue region) into another medium characterized by $\varepsilon^> = \varepsilon_t$ (grey region). The thin film (dashed line) models the electromagnetic response of the ensemble of particles embedded in the transmission medium through the total excess polarization $\vb{P}^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel)$ (represented by the red blurred area) at $z=0$. The reflected  $\vb{E}^{(j)}_\text{r}$ and transmitted electric field $\vb{E}^{(j)}_\text{t}$ for a $j$ polarization state travel at the $\vu{k}_\text{r}$ and $\vu{k}_\text{t}$ direction, respectively, that is, at the reflection $\theta_r$ and transmission angle $\theta_t$.}
	\label{fig:esquemaTFT}
\end{figure}

In general, when an electromagnetic plane wave with an angular frequency $\omega$ travels from a substrate to a thin film in the direction $\vu{k}^\text{inc}$ at an angle $\theta_i$, relative to the normal direction to the thin film, the reflected (transmitted) plane wave does it in the direction of $\vu{k}^\text{r}$ ($\vu{k}^\text{t}$) at the angle $\theta_r$ ($\theta_t$). Thus, the wave vectors of the electromagnetic plane waves are
%
\begin{subequations}%
\begin{align}
	\vb{k}^\text{inc} &= n_i\frac{\omega}{c} (\sin\theta_i \,\vu{e}_x+ \cos\theta_i\, \vu{e}_\perp),\\
%	
	\vb{k}^\text{r} &= n_i \frac{\omega}{c} (\sin\theta_r\, \vu{e}_x- \cos\theta_r\, \vu{e}_\perp),\\
%	
	\vb{k}^\text{t} &= n_t \frac{\omega}{c} (\sin\theta_t\, \vu{e}_x+ \cos\theta_t\, \vu{e}_\perp),
\end{align}%
\label{eq:Wavevectors}%
\end{subequations}%
%
where $\vu{e}_\perp = \vu{e}_z$  and $c$ is the speed of light. For an $s$ polarized electromagnetic plane wave [see Fig. \sfref{fig:esquemaTFT}{a)}], the electric field above and below the thin film is
%
\begin{subequations}%
\begin{align}
	\vb{E}^<(\vb{r}) &= \qty[E^\text{inc}\exp(i\vb{k}^\text{inc}\cdot\vb{r}) +
	E_\text{r}^{(s)}\exp(i\vb{k}^\text{r}\cdot\vb{r})]\vu{e}_y,\\
	%	
	\vb{E}^>(\vb{r}) &= E_\text{t}^{(s)}\exp(i\vb{k}^\text{t}\cdot\vb{r})
	\vu{e}_y,					     
\end{align}\label{eq:EsTFT}%
\end{subequations}%
%
while for a $p$ polarized plane wave, the electric field is given by
%
\begin{subequations}%
\begin{align}
	\vb{E}^<(\vb{r}) =& \qty[-E^\text{inc}\cos\theta_i\exp(i\vb{k}^\text{inc}\cdot\vb{r})
	+E_\text{r}^{(p)}\cos\theta_r\exp(i\vb{k}^\text{r}\cdot\vb{r})
	] \vu{e}_x+ \notag \\
	& +
	\qty[E^\text{inc}\sin\theta_i\exp(i\vb{k}^\text{inc}\cdot\vb{r})
	+E^{(p)}_\text{r}\sin\theta_r\exp(i\vb{k}^\text{r}\cdot\vb{r})
	] \vu{e}_\perp, \\
	%	
	\vb{E}^>(\vb{r}) =& \qty[-E^{(p)}_\text{t}\cos\theta_t\exp(i\vb{k}^\text{t}\cdot\vb{r})
	] \vu{e}_x +
	\qty[-E^{(p)}_\text{t}\sin\theta_t\exp(i\vb{k}^\text{t}\cdot\vb{r})
	] \vu{e}_\perp, 				    
\end{align}\label{eq:EpTFT}%
\end{subequations}%
%
as it can be seen in Fig. \sfref{fig:esquemaTFT}{b)}. In Eqs. \eqref{eq:EsTFT} and \eqref{eq:EpTFT}, the term $E_\xi^{(j)}$ is the magnitude of either the reflected ($\xi=\text{t}$) or transmitted ($\xi=\text{t}$) electric field considering the $j$ polarization state; for either case $E^\text{inc}$ is the magnitude of the incident electric field. Additionally, the incident $\vb{B}^\text{inc}(\vb{r})$, and the reflected and transmitted magnetic fields $\vb{B}_\xi^{(j)}(\vb{r})$ for both polarizations can be determined by substituting Eqs. \eqref{eq:EsTFT} and \eqref{eq:EpTFT} into Faraday-Lenz's Law, while the displacement field and the $H$ field are calculated via the bulk contitutive relations $\vb{D}=\varepsilon\vb{E}(\vb{r})$ and $\vb{H}(\vb{r})=\vb{B}(\vb{r})/\mu_0$ considering each medium linear, homogeneous and isotropic. Prior calculation of the total polarization in the film $\vb{P}^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel)$ it is noteworthy to show that forcing the proposed electric fields in Eqs. \eqref{eq:EsTFT} and \eqref{eq:EpTFT} to follow the boundary conditions shown in Eqs. \eqref{eq:generalBC} yields the continuity of the parallel component of the wave vector $\vb{k}_\parallel = (\omega/c)n_i\sin\,\theta_i \vu{e}_x= (\omega/c)n_t\sin\theta_t\,\vu{e}_x$, therefore stating that even with the presence of the thin film, the reflection and Snell's law are valid \cite{lazzari_granfilm_2002,bedeaux_optical_2004}. Under this consideration, $\vb{P}^\text{film}(\vb{r}_\parallel)$ is obtained by introducing Eqs. \eqref{eq:EsTFT} and \eqref{eq:EpTFT} into Eq. \eqref{eq:P-TFT} yielding
%
\begin{subequations}%
\begin{align}
	\vb{P}^{(s),\text{film}}_\parallel(\vb{r}_\parallel) &=
				 \frac{\gamma}{2}\qty(E^\text{inc} + E_\text{r}^{(s)} + E_\text{t}^{(s)})\exp(\vb{k}_\parallel\cdot\vb{r}) \vu{e}_x,
	\\
	P_\perp^{(s),\text{film}}(\vb{r}_\parallel) &= 0,
\end{align}%
\label{eq:Pfilms}%
\end{subequations}%
%
for $s$ polarization and
%
\begin{subequations}%
\begin{align}
	\vb{P}^{(p),\text{film}}_\parallel(\vb{r}_\parallel) &= 
					\frac{\gamma}{2}\qty[
					\qty(E_\text{r}^{(p)}-E^\text{inc})\cos\theta_i
						-E_\text{t}^{(p)}\cos\theta_t
				] \exp(\vb{k}_\parallel\cdot\vb{r}) \vu{e}_x,
	\\
	P_\perp^{(p),\text{film}}(\vb{r}_\parallel)  &= \frac{\beta}{2}\qty[			
				\varepsilon_i\qty(E^\text{inc}+E_\text{r}^{(p)})\sin\theta_i 
				+ \varepsilon_t E_\text{t}^{(p)} \sin\theta_t ] \exp(\vb{k}_\parallel\cdot\vb{r}),
\end{align}%
\label{eq:Pfilmp}%
\end{subequations}%
%
for $p$ polarization.

As stated above, the TFT returns expressions for reflection and transmission amplitude coefficients. These coefficients are obtained for an $s$ polarized incident plane wave by solving the  system of equations obtained by substituting Eqs. \eqref{eq:Wavevectors}, \eqref{eq:EsTFT} and \eqref{eq:Pfilms} into the boundary conditions in Eq. \eqref{eq:generalBC}, yielding
%
\begin{subequations}%
\begin{align}
	E^\text{inc} + E^{(s)}_\text{r}  & =  E^{(s)}_\text{t}   ,\\
	\qty(n_i\cos\theta_i+ \frac{i}{2}\frac{\omega}{c}\gamma)E^\text{inc}
	- \qty(n_i\cos\theta_i- \frac{i}{2}\frac{\omega}{c}\gamma)E^{(s)}_\text{r}
	&= \qty(n_t\cos\theta_t- \frac{i}{2}\frac{\omega}{c}\gamma)E^{(s)}_\text{t}.
\end{align}%
\label{eq:Systems}%
\end{subequations}%
%
Analogously, the system of equations for a $p$ polarized incident plane wave obtained from Eqs. \eqref{eq:generalBC}, \eqref{eq:Wavevectors} \eqref{eq:EpTFT} and \eqref{eq:Pfilmp} is
%
\begin{subequations}%
\begin{align}
			\hspace*{-1em}\qty(\cos\theta_i + ik_\parallel\frac{\beta}{2}\varepsilon_i\sin\theta_i)E^\text{inc} 
			- \qty(\cos\theta_i - ik_\parallel\frac{\beta}{2}\varepsilon_i\sin\theta_i)E^{(p)}_\text{r}
			&= \qty(\cos\theta_t -ik_\parallel\frac{\beta}{2}\varepsilon_t\sin\theta_t) E^{(p)}_\text{t} ,\\
%	
			\qty(n_i + ik_\parallel\frac{\gamma}{2}\cos\theta_i)E^\text{inc} 
			+ \qty(n_i - ik_\parallel\frac{\gamma}{2}\cos\theta_i)E^{(p)}_\text{r}  
			&= \qty(n_t - ik_\parallel\frac{\gamma}{2}\cos\theta_t) E^{(p)}_\text{t} ,
\end{align}%
\label{eq:Systemp}%
\end{subequations}
%
with $k_\parallel = (\omega/c)n_i\sin\theta_i =(\omega/c)n_t\sin\theta_t$. Thus, the reflection and transmission amplitude coefficients given by the solution to Eqs. \eqref{eq:Systems} for $\xi ^{(s)}_\text{TFT} = E_\xi^{(s)}/E^\text{inc}$ are \cite{lazzari_granfilm_2002,bedeaux_optical_2004,cuanalo_phase_2024}
%
\begin{subequations}%
{\allowdisplaybreaks\begin{align}
	r^{(s)}_\text{TFT}(\theta_i) =& 
				\frac{ n_i \cos\theta_i - n_t\cos\theta_t + i (\omega/c)\gamma }
			   		 { n_i \cos\theta_i + n_t\cos\theta_t - i (\omega/c)\gamma },
	\label{eq:rsTFT} \\
	t^{(s)}_\text{TFT}(\theta_i) =& 
				\frac{ 2 n_i \cos\theta_i}
					 { n_i \cos\theta_i + n_t\cos\theta_t - i (\omega/c)\gamma },
\end{align}}%
\label{eq:rtsTFT}%
\end{subequations}
%
while for $\xi ^{(p)}_\text{TFT} = E_\xi^{(p)}/E^\text{inc}$, obtained by solving Eqs. \eqref{eq:Systemp}, the amplitude coefficients are \cite{lazzari_granfilm_2002,bedeaux_optical_2004,cuanalo_phase_2024}
%
\begin{subequations}%
\begin{align}
	r^{(p)}_\text{TFT}(\theta_i) &= 
		  \frac{ \kappa_-(\omega) - i (\omega/c) \gamma\cos\theta_i\cos\theta_t +
							i (\omega/c)  n_i n_t \varepsilon_i \beta \sin^2 \theta_i }
			   { \kappa_+(\omega) - i (\omega/c) \gamma\cos\theta_i\cos\theta_t -
			   				i (\omega/c)  n_i n_t \varepsilon_i \beta \sin^2 \theta_i },
	\label{eq:rpTFT}\\
	t^{(p)}_\text{TFT}(\theta_i) &= 
		  \frac{ n_i \cos\theta_i \qty(1-\qty(\omega/2c)^2\varepsilon_{i}\gamma\beta \sin^{2}\theta_i) }
				{ \kappa_+(\omega) - i (\omega/c) \gamma\cos\theta_i\cos\theta_t -
							i (\omega/c)  n_i n_t \varepsilon_i \beta \sin^2 \theta_i },
				\\	
	\kappa_{\pm}(\omega) &= \bigg( n_t \cos\theta_i \pm n_i \cos\theta_t \bigg)
							\qty[1-\qty(\frac{\omega}{2c})^2\varepsilon_{i}\gamma\beta \sin^{2}\theta_i].
\end{align}%
\label{eq:rtpTFT}%
\end{subequations}%

To completely describe the optical properties of a metasurface of identical plasmonic scatterers supported by a substrate with Eqs. \eqref{eq:rtsTFT} and \eqref{eq:rtpTFT}, the film susceptibilities $\gamma$ and $\beta$ defined in Eq. \eqref{eq:P-TFT} must be specified. When considering spherical scatterers of radius $a$ embedded in the transmission medium and supported on a planar sharp interface with the incidence medium, the electromagnetic response of the ensemble can be modeled by electric point dipoles and their images if the condition $n_ta\omega/c \ll 1$ is fulfilled \cite{lazzari_granfilm_2002}. Under these considerations in addition to setting the thickness of the thin film modeling the ensemble of particles as $2a$, the film susceptibilities are given by \cite{lazzari_granfilm_2002,bedeaux_optical_2004,haarmans_optical_1995}
%
\begin{align}
	\gamma = \frac23 \frac{\Theta}{V_\text{sp}} \qty( \frac{\alpha_\text{sp}}{1 + A \alpha_\text{sp}})
	\qquad\text{and}\qquad
	 \beta =\frac23 \frac{\Theta}{\varepsilon_i^2 V_\text{sp}} \qty(\frac{\alpha_\text{sp}}{1 + 2 A \alpha_\text{sp}})
\label{eq:gammabeta}
\end{align}
%
where $\Theta$ is the surface coverage of the particles ensemble on the substrate and where $\alpha_\text{sp}$ and $A$ are the polarizability of the spherical particles and the image strength, respectively, defined as
%
\begin{align}
	\alpha_\text{sp} = 3  \varepsilon_t V_\text{sp} \qty(\frac{\varepsilon_\text{sp} - \varepsilon_t}{\varepsilon_\text{sp} + 2 \varepsilon_t}) 
	\qquad\text{and}\qquad
	A = \frac{\varepsilon_i - \varepsilon_t}{\varepsilon_i + \varepsilon_t},
	\label{eq:alpha-A}
\end{align}
%
with  $\varepsilon_\text{sp}$ the dielectric function of the spheres and $V_\text{sp}$ their volume. It is noteworthy that  the expressions of $r_\text{TFT}$ and $t_\text{TFT}$ shown in the Eqs. \eqref{eq:rtsTFT} and \eqref{eq:rtpTFT} describe the optical properties of the ensemble when the particles are embedded in the transmission medium. By interchanging $\varepsilon_t \leftrightarrow \varepsilon_i$ and $n_ t \leftrightarrow n_i$ in Eqs. \eqref{eq:rtsTFT}--\eqref{eq:alpha-A}, the amplitude coefficients describe the scenario when the particles are embedded in the medium of incidence~\cite{bedeaux_optical_2004}.
