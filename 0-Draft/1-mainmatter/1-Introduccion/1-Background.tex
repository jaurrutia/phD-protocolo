\section*{Background}

Optical metasurfaces are bidimensional ensembles of metallic or dielectric nanostructures tailored to  exhibit optical responses not found in naturally occurring materials when illuminated at selected wavelengths \cite{khan_optical_2022,gonzalez-alcalde_large_2020}. Their optical properties are determined by the physical characteristics of their constituent nanostructures, such as size, shape, orientation, and spatial distribution within the ensemble \cite{kim_plasmonic_2019,khan_optical_2022}. The tunability achieved through these design parameters enables precise spatial control of light, positioning metasurfaces as versatile tools for applications in spectroscopy \cite{khan_optical_2022}, color generation \cite{gonzalez-alcalde_large_2020}, optical communications \cite{chen_review_2016}, and optical sensing \cite{estevez_trends_2014,jain_noble_2008,kim_plasmonic_2019}.

In recent years, the use of metasurfaces to biomedical and diagnostic technologies has gain interest due to their potential for highly sensitive, real-time, and label-free detection \cite{estevez_trends_2014,kabashin_label-free_2023}. Particularly, plasmonic metasurfaces have proven particularly effective as refractive index sensors for biomolecular detection  \cite{kabashin_label-free_2023,kabashin_plasmonic_2009,khan_optical_2022,cuanalo_phase_2024,moirangthem_enhanced_2012,feuz_improving_2010,kabashin_plasmonic_2009,svedendahl_refractometric_2014,qiu_dual_2020}. For instance, \citeauthor*{kabashin_plasmonic_2009}~\cite{kabashin_plasmonic_2009} designed a biosensing-aimed metasurfaces consisting of an ordered array of cylindrical gold (Au) nanoparticles (NPs) that detects refractive index changes via spectral shifts in reflectivity minima [Fig.  \sfref{fig:Back}{a)}]. More recently, \citeauthor*{qiu_dual_2020}~\cite{qiu_dual_2020} employed a disordered metasurfaces of spherical AuNPs to detect SARS-CoV-2 DNA sequences by monitoring phase shifts in the reflected light with the Localized Surface Plasmon Resonance (LSPR) response [Fig.  \sfref{fig:Back}{b)}]. The two theoretical-experimental works presented in Fig.  \ref{fig:Back} are not only examples of different kinds of metasurfaces, but also of different sensing mechanisms: one based on reflectivity \cite{kabashin_plasmonic_2009} and the other on ellipsometric \cite{qiu_dual_2020} measurements. The design of a metasurface and the choice of its sensing mechanism considers not only fabrication feasibility but also the targeted optical response, which can be predicted through different theoretical approaches. 

In terms of fabrication, the disordered metasurfaces of spheroidal NPs have  gained attention due to their ease of synthesis through scalable techniques such as thermal dewetting \cite{wang_formation_2011,cuanalo_phase_2024} and laser ablation \cite{hammad_improving_2023}. In particular, the dewetting is a fabrication method, which consists in thermally annihilating metallic thin films to synthesize spheroidal NPs \cite{wang_formation_2011}, which exhibit broad size distributions and random spatial arrangements \cite{hammad_improving_2023,wang_formation_2011}. Fig. \sfref{fig:Subs}{a)} presents   Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) images ans size distributions of synthesized AuNPs ensembles fabricated by dewetting Au films with a thickness of $5$~nm (in red) and a $60$~nm (in blue). As exemplified with  Fig. \sfref{fig:Subs}{a)}, the dewetting is a method that reduce production cost and time \cite{cuanalo_phase_2024}, specially when compared to lithography techniques for ordered metasurfaces [\textbf{REF}]. Fabrication techniques like dewetting enablesd large-area fabrication, making disordered metasurfaces attractive for practical biosensing applications \cite{qiu_dual_2020,wang_formation_2011,cuanalo_phase_2024,moirangthem_enhanced_2012}.

The theoretical response of metasurfaces aimed to biosensign have been determined with both numerical and analytical methods. Numerical simulations based on the Finite Element Method (FEM) \cite{feuz_improving_2010,borah_plasmon_2022} or the Finite-Difference Time-Domain (FDTD) method \cite{qiu_differential_2015} have been widely used for periodic structures. However, these methods are computationally demanding when applied to disordered systems \cite{tyson_new_2020,qiu_differential_2015}. To address disordered meatasurfaces analytically, two primarily categories arises: Effective Medium Theories (EMTs) and 
Multiple Scattering Theories (MSTs). Some EMTs used to model metasurfaces conformed of  spheroidal particles are the Maxwell Garnett Model \cite{reyes2018analytical} and the Bruggeman Formula \cite{bedeaux_optical_2004}, whose derivation considers colloidal 3D systems under the quasistatic approximation \cite{sihvola_electromagnetic_2008}. While phenomenological modification have been implemented to suit these EMTs to metasurfaces \cite{kabashin_plasmonic_2009}, the MSTs provide a more rigorous and realistic framework by modeling the metasurface as a discrete collection of interacting particles up to different orders of approximation \cite{loiko_monolayers_1998}.  The Coherent Scattering Model (CSM) \cite{reyes2018analytical} and the Thin Film Theory (TFT) \cite{bedeaux_optical_2004} are two MSTs capable of accounting for realistic fabrication byproducts, such as size distributions and substrate interactions \cite{vazquez-estrada_optical_2014,bedeaux_optical_2004}.

As stated above, metasurfaces farbicated by dewetting can exhibit a borad size distribution of the syntghezies NPs and a random spatial arrange of them \cite{hammad_improving_2023,wang_formation_2011}. Although both the CSM and TFT are suitable for describing these systems, certain fabrication-related effects ---such as partial embedding of NPs into the substrate--- are not yet fully considered by current MSTs. This partial embedding, observed for specific combinations of film thickness and annealing temperature \cite{moirangthem_enhanced_2012,meng_anisotropic_2015}, can significantly alter the optical properties, as evidenced by spectral shifts in experimental extinction spectra [Fig.  \sfref{fig:Subs}{b)}]. Despite attempts to account for substrate effects using phenomenological EMT-based fittings, as illustrated in the inset of Fig.  \sfref{fig:Subs}{b)}, a comprehensive theoretical understanding of this kind of substrate interactions in disordered metasurfaces remains lacking.

While the influence of the substrate on single NPs has been rigorously studied [\textbf{ref}], its impact on ensembles becomes a less explored area. Numerical methods have been proved useful for these studies when applied to ordered systems. For  instance,  FEM simulations  ---and their respective experimental corroboration---  were conducted by \citeauthor*{borah_plasmon_2022}  \cite{borah_plasmon_2022}  for AuNPs in an hexagonal array, reporting the effect on the  optical response of the ensemble due to its density, illumination scheme and even the effect of the substrate when illuminated. In particular,  Fig. \sfref{fig:Subs}{c)} depicts the substrate effect by sowing the reflectivity and near-field enhancement maxima spectra  of a closed packed hexagonal AuNPs array illuminated at normal incidents when a substrate is considered (disk markers) or not (continuous lines). However, analogous studies for disordered arrays are scarce, leaving open questions about how substrate interactions, including partial embedding, influence the collective optical response and how these effects impact the design and performance of metasurfaces for sensing applications.
