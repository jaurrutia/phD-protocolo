\section*{Background}

Optical metasurfaces are bidimensional ensembles of metallic or dielectric nanostructures tailored to exhibit optical responses, when illuminated at specific wavelengths, not found in nature by simple materials \cite{khan_optical_2022,gonzalez-alcalde_large_2020}. Their optical properties are determined by the physical characteristics of their conforming nanostructures, such as size, shape, orientation, and spatial distribution within the ensemble \cite{kim_plasmonic_2019,khan_optical_2022}. The tunability achieved by selecting the aforementioned parameters allows metasurfaces to control light in space with high precision, enabling applications in areas such as spectroscopy \cite{khan_optical_2022}, color structuration \cite{gonzalez-alcalde_large_2020}, communications \cite{chen_review_2016}, and sensing \cite{estevez_trends_2014,jain_noble_2008,khan_optical_2022,chen_review_2016,kim_plasmonic_2019}.

In recent years, the use of metasurfaces to biomedical and diagnostic technologies has gain interest due to their potential for highly sensitive, real-time, and label-free detection \cite{estevez_trends_2014,kabashin_label-free_2023}. Particularly, plasmonic metasurfaces have shown promise as refractive index sensors for biomolecular detection \cite{kabashin_label-free_2023,kabashin_plasmonic_2009,khan_optical_2022,cuanalo_phase_2024,moirangthem_enhanced_2012,feuz_improving_2010,kabashin_plasmonic_2009,svedendahl_refractometric_2014,qiu_dual_2020}. For example, in 2009 \citeauthor*{kabashin_plasmonic_2009}~\cite{kabashin_plasmonic_2009} designed a biosensing-aimed metasurfaces consisting in an ordered array of cylindrical gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) [see Fig. \sfref{fig:Back}{a)}], which determined the refractive index change by identiying the spectral shift in me minima of reflectivity spectra when illuminating the system. More recently, in 2020 \citeauthor*{qiu_dual_2020}~\cite{qiu_dual_2020} employed disordered metasurfaces of spherical AuNPs, which measured thiol-cDNA concentrations, for SARS-CoV-2 detection, by tracking the phase response of the  Localized Surface Plasmon Resonance (LSPR) excited on the AuNPs, as shown in Fig. \sfref{fig:Back}{b)}. When compairing ordered metasurfaces to disordered systems of AuNPs with spheroidal geometries, the later are particularly attractive due to their fabrication processes, such as thermal dewetting \cite{qiu_dual_2020,wang_formation_2011,cuanalo_phase_2024,moirangthem_enhanced_2012}, which reduce production costs and fabrication time, and enhance scalability \cite{cuanalo_phase_2024}.

The design of a metasurface not only takes into account the technical capability for its synthesis, but also the desired optical response, which can be theoretically predicted by either numerical or  analytical approaches.  For metasurfaces presenting  periodicity within its nanostructuration, the Finite Element Method (FEM) has been used \cite{feuz_improving_2010,michieli_optimal_2020}, however, numerical approaches used for disordered metasurfaces exhibit a high demand in computation time and resources \cite{tyson_new_2020,qiu_differential_2015}. To address the optical properties for disordered systems analytically, there are two main approaches: Effective Medium Theories (EMTs) and Multiple Scattering Theories (MSTs). Some EMT used to model metasurfaces conformed of  spheroidal particles are the Maxwell Garnett Model \cite{reyes2018analytical} and the Bruggeman Formula \cite{bedeaux_optical_2004}, whose derivation considers colloidal 3D systems under the quasistatic approximation \cite{sihvola_electromagnetic_2008}. While phenomenological modificatin have been implemented to suit this EMT to metasurfaces \cite{kabashin_plasmonic_2009}, the MST become more appropriate. In particular, MSTs like Coherent Scattering Model (CSM) \cite{reyes2018analytical} and the Thin Film Theory (TFT) \cite{bedeaux_optical_2004}  provides a more rigorous and realistic framework by modeling the metasurface as a discrete collection of interacting particles up to different orders of approximation \cite{loiko_monolayers_1998}. 
 
  These models assume idealized conditions such as subwavelength particle sizes and narrow size distributions However, metasurfaces fabricated by physical methods like thermal dewetting or laser ablation typically exhibit broad size distributions and random spatial arrangements that violate these assumptions \cite{hammad_improving_2023,wang_formation_2011,vazquez-estrada_optical_2014}. Under such conditions, where the small-particle approximation fails, Multiple Scattering Theories (MSTs) treats the metasurface as a discrete ensemble of interacting nanoparticles, enabling it to account for size distributions, near-field interactions, and substrate effects.
